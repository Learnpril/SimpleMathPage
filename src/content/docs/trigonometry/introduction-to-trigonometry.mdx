## What You'll Learn

In this first Trigonometry lesson you'll learn the three primary trigonometric ratios - sine, cosine, and tangent - and how to use them to relate the angles and sides of right triangles.

## The Concept

Trigonometry is the study of the relationships between the angles and sides of triangles. Most of the beginning work focuses on **right triangles** (triangles with one 90° angle).

For any acute angle θ (theta) in a right triangle, we define three important ratios.

_(As a side note, θ is a Greek letter that mathematicians use as a standard name for angles. You'll see it everywhere in trigonometry.)_

**Sine** of θ:

$$
\sin(\theta) = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}
$$

**Cosine** of θ:

$$
\cos(\theta) = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}
$$

**Tangent** of θ:

$$
\tan(\theta) = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}
$$

These are commonly remembered with the mnemonic **SOH-CAH-TOA** (we covered this in the intro page, but it's worth repeating because it's that important):

- **SOH** → Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
- **CAH** → Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
- **TOA** → Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

The **hypotenuse** is always the side opposite the right angle (the longest side). The **opposite** side is the one directly across from angle θ. The **adjacent** side is the one next to angle θ (not the hypotenuse).

## Worked Example

Consider a right triangle where angle θ = 30°, the side opposite θ is 5 units, and the hypotenuse is 10 units.

import Trig30Triangle from "../../../components/Trig30Triangle.astro";

<Trig30Triangle />

**1. Find sin(30°)**

Since we're finding sine, we use SOH: Opposite / Hypotenuse.

$$
\sin(30°) = \frac{5}{10} = 0.5
$$

**2. Find the adjacent side using the Pythagorean Theorem**

$$
\text{Adjacent} = \sqrt{10^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{100 - 25} = \sqrt{75} \approx 8.66
$$

**3. Find cos(30°) and tan(30°)**

$$
\cos(30°) \approx \frac{8.66}{10} = 0.866
$$

$$
\tan(30°) \approx \frac{5}{8.66} \approx 0.577
$$

## Real-World Application

Right triangle trigonometry is used in many practical fields:

- Construction and roofing (calculating rafter lengths and slopes)
- Navigation and surveying (measuring distances you can't walk directly)
- Engineering (designing ramps, bridges, and structures with specific angles)
- Physics (resolving forces into components)
- Everyday tasks (finding the height of a tree or building using a simple angle measurement)

Example: a ladder leans against a wall forming a 68° angle with the ground. Using trigonometry, you can calculate how high up the wall the ladder reaches if you know the ladder's length.

:::note[You've Got This]
Trigonometry starts with three simple ratios that relate the sides of a right triangle to its angles. SOH-CAH-TOA makes them easy to remember. Draw the triangle and clearly label the opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse sides for each angle. That visual step makes everything much clearer.
:::
