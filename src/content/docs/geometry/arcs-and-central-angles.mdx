## What You'll Learn

In this lesson you'll learn what arcs and central angles are, how they relate to each other, and how to find their measures in a circle.

## The Concept

A **central angle** is an angle whose vertex is at the center of the circle and whose sides pass through two points on the circle.

An **arc** is the curved portion of the circle between those two points.

Key relationships:

- The measure of a **minor arc** (the shorter arc) is equal to the measure of its central angle.
- The measure of a **major arc** (the longer arc) is 360° minus the measure of the minor arc.
- The entire circle is 360°.

## Worked Example

1. A central angle measures 90°. What is the measure of the minor arc?

   90° (same as the central angle).

2. A central angle is 120°. What is the measure of the major arc?

   360° − 120° = 240°.

3. The minor arc between points A and B measures 75°. What is the central angle?

   75° (they are equal).

## Real-World Application

Arcs and central angles are used in many practical situations:

- Cutting a pizza or cake into equal slices (each slice is defined by a central angle and its arc).
- Designing circular gardens, fountains, or patios with specific curved sections.
- Navigation: GPS and maps often describe turns as arcs with central angles.
- Sports: The path of a thrown ball or the curve of a racetrack.
- Architecture: Arched doorways, windows, or bridges use arc measurements.

For example, if you want to divide a circular flower bed into 5 equal sections, each central angle would be 360° ÷ 5 = 72°, and each arc would also measure 72°.

:::note[You've Got This]
The measure of a minor arc is exactly the same as its central angle — that's the key shortcut. For major arcs, subtract from 360°. Look at round objects like clocks, pizzas, or wheels and imagine the central angles that create different arcs. It becomes very intuitive with a little practice.
:::
