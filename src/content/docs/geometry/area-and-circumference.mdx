## What You'll Learn

In this lesson you'll learn the formulas for circumference and area of a circle and how to use them in real situations.

## The Concept

**Circumference** is the distance around the circle (the perimeter of the circle).

$$
C = 2\pi r \quad \text{or} \quad C = \pi d
$$

**Area** is the space inside the circle.

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

Where:

- $r$ = radius
- $d$ = diameter
- $\pi \approx 3.14$ (or leave it as $\pi$ for exact answers)

<CircumferenceArea />

Important notes:

- Circumference uses the radius or diameter (linear measurement).
- Area uses the radius squared (square units).

## Worked Example

**1. A circle has a radius of 5 cm.**

<CircleR5Example />

Circumference = $2\pi \times 5 = 10\pi$ cm ≈ 31.4 cm

Area = $\pi \times 5^2 = 25\pi$ cm² ≈ 78.5 cm²

**2. A circle has a diameter of 12 inches.**

Radius = 6 inches

Circumference = $\pi \times 12 = 12\pi$ inches ≈ 37.7 inches

Area = $\pi \times 6^2 = 36\pi$ in² ≈ 113.1 in²

**3. A circle has circumference 62.8 cm. What is the radius?**

$C = 2\pi r$ → $62.8 = 2\pi r$ → $r = 62.8 \div (2\pi) \approx 10$ cm

## Real-World Application

Circumference and area are used all the time:

- Buying fencing for a circular garden or yard (circumference).
- Calculating how much paint or fertilizer you need for a circular area.
- Determining the size of a round tablecloth or rug.
- Engineering: Tire size, pipe capacity, or wheel rotations (distance = circumference × number of turns).
- Cooking: How much dough you need for a round pizza or cake pan.

Example: You want to put a fence around a circular pond with radius 8 feet. Circumference tells you how much fencing to buy: $2\pi \times 8 \approx 50.3$ feet.

:::note[You've Got This]
Remember: Circumference is around the circle (uses 2πr or πd), area is inside the circle (πr²). Use the radius for both when possible. Practice with real round objects (a plate, a wheel, or a pizza) and calculate their circumference and area. It makes the formulas feel useful and concrete.
:::
