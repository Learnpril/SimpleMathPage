## What You'll Learn

In this lesson you'll learn standard form of a linear equation, why it's useful, and how to convert between slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard forms.

## The Concept

**Standard form** is Ax + By = C, where A, B, and C are integers, A &ge; 0, and A and B are not both zero. It's useful for:

- Systems of equations (easier to solve with elimination)
- Finding intercepts quickly (x-intercept: C/A when y = 0; y-intercept: C/B when x = 0)
- Keeping coefficients integer with no fractions

Conversions:

- Slope-intercept y = mx + b → standard: mx − y = −b (multiply by −1 if needed for A &gt; 0)
- Point-slope y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) → distribute m, move terms to one side, clear fractions if needed
- Standard → slope-intercept: solve for y (isolate y term)

Example conversions:

1. y = 3x − 5 → 3x − y = 5
2. y − 4 = −2(x + 1) → y − 4 = −2x − 2 → y + 2x = 2 → 2x + y = 2
3. 4x − 5y = 20 → −5y = −4x + 20 → y = (4/5)x − 4 (slope-intercept)

## Worked Example

Convert y = −(3/2)x + 6 to standard form.

1. y + (3/2)x = 6
2. Multiply by 2 to clear fraction: 2y + 3x = 12
3. 3x + 2y = 12 (A = 3 &gt; 0)

Another: Convert point-slope y − 7 = 4(x − 2) to standard.

1. y − 7 = 4x − 8
2. y = 4x − 1
3. −4x + y = −1 → 4x − y = 1

## Real-World Application

Standard form is common in budgeting, business, or planning:

- Budget constraint: 3x + 5y = 150 (3 dollars food + 5 dollars gas = 150 dollars total budget)
- Two plans: 4x + 2y = 100 and 5x + 3y = 120 (solve system for break-even)
- Linear programming (intro): constraints in standard form help find max/min

Converting forms lets you pick the best view: slope-intercept for rate/start, standard for intercepts/systems.

:::note[You've Got This]
Standard form is just another way to write the same line. No fractions, integer coefficients. To convert, move terms around and clear denominators. Practice switching between forms with real constraints (budgets, plans) and you'll see which form makes the problem easiest.
:::
