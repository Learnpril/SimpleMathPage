This is a statement of intent and a list of known gaps. It is **not** a
conformance claim. Nobody has audited this site with real assistive technology,
so anything below that sounds like a promise is only a description of what the
code currently does.

## What this site is aiming for

Usable with a keyboard alone. Readable without fighting the contrast. Math and
figures that a screen reader can reach, rather than pictures of equations that
are invisible to anything but an eye.

The last one is the reason for most of the technical decisions here. A math site
that ships equations as images is unusable to a screen reader and unreadable when
zoomed, and that is a very easy mistake to make.

## What is in place today

Verifiable by reading the built pages:

- **Equations are markup, not pictures.** Math is rendered by KaTeX, which emits
  MathML alongside the visual layout and hides the visual copy from assistive
  technology. The original TeX travels with it. Zooming enlarges real text.
- **Figures are SVG drawn from computed values.** Labels inside them are real
  text that scales and can be selected, not baked-in pixels.
- **Every figure has a text description.** All 379 figure components carry an
  accessible name, so a screen reader announces what the diagram shows rather
  than skipping it. Purely decorative icons are hidden from assistive technology
  instead, so they are not read out as meaningless graphics.
- **Quizzes work from the keyboard.** Each question is a labelled radio group:
  arrow keys move between options, Enter or Space selects, and the result is
  announced through a live region rather than by colour alone.
- **A skip-to-content link** is the first thing in the tab order on every page.
- **Tap targets are 44px** in the mobile navigation drawer and the footer links.
- **Dark theme by default, light theme available**, and the choice is remembered.
- **Nothing plays sound on its own.** The alma mater is a button in the header,
  off on every page load, and never resumes by itself.
- Pages declare their language, and headings run in order rather than being
  chosen for their size.

## What is not done

The honest list, and it is the more useful half of this page:

- **Some figures share one description between several panels.** Where a figure
  is a row of two or three small graphs, each panel now carries its own label,
  but a handful of grouped figures elsewhere still describe the whole group in
  one sentence rather than each part.
- **The descriptions vary in quality.** Every figure has one. Some are a full
  sentence explaining what the diagram shows and why; others are a short phrase
  that names the picture without teaching what it demonstrates. The short ones
  need rewriting, and that is a slow job done by hand.
- **No reduced-motion handling.** Nothing in the stylesheet responds to
  `prefers-reduced-motion` yet, so animated and interactive figures ignore that
  preference.
- **Contrast has not been measured.** The palette was chosen by eye. It has not
  been checked against a contrast ratio for every combination, particularly
  inside figures.
- **No screen reader testing.** Not with NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver or anything else.
  The markup was written to be reachable; that is not the same as confirming it
  reads well.
- **Interactive 3D figures have no non-visual equivalent.** Where a lesson leans
  on one to make its point, that point is currently harder to get at.

## No conformance claim

This site does not claim to conform to WCAG at any level. Making that claim
properly needs testing with real assistive technology and expert review, and
neither has happened here. Claiming it without that would be a nicer sentence
and a worse one.

If that matters for your institution, take this page as the honest input to your
own evaluation rather than as a substitute for it.

## If something blocks you

Email <a href={`mailto:${CONTACT_EMAIL}`}>{CONTACT_EMAIL}</a>. Tell me the page
and what happened - "the quiz on this lesson will not take my keyboard" is
plenty, and your browser and assistive software help if you know them.

Accessibility bugs go in front of new lessons. This is one person working around
a job, so the queue is real, but a barrier is not the same as a feature request
and it does not sit in the same line.
