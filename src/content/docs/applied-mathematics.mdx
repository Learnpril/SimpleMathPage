Math aimed at a craft. Where [Core Mathematics](/core-mathematics/) builds the
subject from the ground up and proves things, this track starts from a thing you
want to build and works backwards to the math that makes it possible.

The difference is real, not cosmetic. Expect fewer proofs, fewer quizzes, and more
worked projects. Formulas show up here because something on screen needs them, and
every track says plainly which Core subjects it leans on.

You do not need to finish Core first. Most tracks need arithmetic, some algebra and
comfortable trigonometry, and they name any heavier prerequisite where it comes up.

## How this track is different

**Projects instead of problem sets.** A lesson ends with something running, not with
twenty exercises. The check on your understanding is whether the thing behaves.

**Engine-agnostic math, concrete examples.** The mathematics in these tracks does not
belong to any one program. Vectors, matrices, quaternions, noise and lighting models
work the same everywhere. Godot and Blender are used as the main examples because they
are free, easy to get hold of, and good to learn in, but everything here transfers
directly to Unity, Unreal, or an engine you wrote yourself.

Each idea is taught as mathematics first, then shown in a tool, with a note on where
another engine differs when the difference actually matters. Engines come and go; the
math does not.

**Prerequisites stated, not assumed.** Each track opens with the Core subjects it
depends on and links straight to them. If a shader lesson needs the dot product, it
links to the lesson that builds the dot product.

:::note[Want the rigorous version?]
Every technique here rests on something proved in
[Core Mathematics](/core-mathematics/). If a shortcut here leaves you wanting the
real reason it works, that is exactly what Core is for.
:::
