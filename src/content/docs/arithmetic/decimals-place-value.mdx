## What You'll Learn

In this lesson you'll learn what decimal numbers represent, how to read and write them correctly, and the place value of digits after the decimal point (tenths, hundredths, thousandths).

## The Concept

**Decimals** are another way to show parts of a whole — like fractions but using powers of 10. The decimal point separates whole numbers from fractional parts.

Place value after the decimal (left to right):

- Tenths (first digit after point): $0.1 = \frac{1}{10}$
- Hundredths (second): $0.01 = \frac{1}{100}$
- Thousandths (third): $0.001 = \frac{1}{1000}$

Example: $3.456$ means:

- 3 whole units
- 4 tenths ($4 \times 0.1 = 0.4$)
- 5 hundredths ($5 \times 0.01 = 0.05$)
- 6 thousandths ($6 \times 0.001 = 0.006$)

Total: $3 + 0.4 + 0.05 + 0.006 = 3.456$

To read: Say "and" at the decimal point, then name the last place.
$3.456$ = "three and four hundred fifty-six thousandths"

## Worked Example

Write and read $0.875$.

- 8 tenths, 7 hundredths, 5 thousandths
- As fraction: $\frac{875}{1000} = \frac{7}{8}$
- Read: "eight hundred seventy-five thousandths"

Compare $0.875$ and $0.87$:

- $0.875$ has 5 thousandths extra → $0.875 > 0.87$

## Real-World Application

Decimals are everywhere in money (42 dollars and 75 cents = 42.75), gas prices (3.89 per gallon), measurements (5.25 feet), or batting averages (0.312). Understanding place value helps you read receipts, price tags, or sports stats accurately without confusion.

:::note[You've Got This]
Decimals are just fractions with powers of 10 — think of the point as a divider. Say the number out loud a few times (like "two point three five" for 2.35) and practice reading prices or measurements around you. It'll feel natural fast.
:::
