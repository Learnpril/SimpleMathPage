## What You'll Learn

Multiplication is just repeated addition, and once you get comfortable with single-digit multiplication (the times tables), everything from multi-digit multiplication to algebra gets easier.

## What Multiplication Means

$3 \times 4$ means "3 groups of 4" or equivalently "add 4 three times":

$$
3 \times 4 = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12
$$

The $\times$ sign means "groups of." You can also read it as "times."

Key ideas:

- **Order doesn't matter**: $3 \times 4 = 4 \times 3 = 12$
- **Multiplying by 1 changes nothing**: $7 \times 1 = 7$
- **Multiplying by 0 always gives 0**: $5 \times 0 = 0$

### The 2s: Just Double It

$2 \times$ anything is the same as adding the number to itself:

- $2 \times 3 = 6$ (double 3)
- $2 \times 7 = 14$ (double 7)
- $2 \times 9 = 18$ (double 9)

### The 5s: Count by Fives

Results always end in 0 or 5:

- $5 \times 1 = 5$
- $5 \times 2 = 10$
- $5 \times 3 = 15$
- $5 \times 4 = 20$

### The 9s Trick

The digits of any 9s product always add up to 9:

- $9 \times 2 = 18$ (1 + 8 = 9)
- $9 \times 3 = 27$ (2 + 7 = 9)
- $9 \times 4 = 36$ (3 + 6 = 9)
- $9 \times 7 = 63$ (6 + 3 = 9)

Also: the tens digit is always one less than what you're multiplying by. $9 \times 7$: tens digit is 6, ones digit is 3 (since 6 + 3 = 9). Answer: 63.

### The 10s: Just Add a Zero

- $10 \times 3 = 30$
- $10 \times 7 = 70$

### Squares (A Number Times Itself)

These come up constantly:

- $1 \times 1 = 1$
- $2 \times 2 = 4$
- $3 \times 3 = 9$
- $4 \times 4 = 16$
- $5 \times 5 = 25$
- $6 \times 6 = 36$
- $7 \times 7 = 49$
- $8 \times 8 = 64$
- $9 \times 9 = 81$

## The Tough Ones

Most people find these the hardest to memorize:

- $6 \times 7 = 42$
- $6 \times 8 = 48$
- $7 \times 8 = 56$
- $8 \times 9 = 72$

A trick for $7 \times 8$: "5, 6, 7, 8" → $56 = 7 \times 8$.

## Worked Examples

**$6 \times 9$**

Use the 9s trick: tens digit = 5 (one less than 6), ones digit = 4 (since 5 + 4 = 9). Answer: 54.

**$8 \times 7$**

If you know $7 \times 7 = 49$, then $8 \times 7 = 49 + 7 = 56$.

**$4 \times 8$**

Double 8 twice: $2 \times 8 = 16$, then $2 \times 16 = 32$. So $4 \times 8 = 32$.

:::tip[Memorize the Times Tables]
There's no shortcut around it. Knowing your times tables cold makes everything from fractions to algebra dramatically easier. Drill them until they're instant.
:::
