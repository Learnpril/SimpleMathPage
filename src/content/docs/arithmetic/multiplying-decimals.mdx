## What You'll Learn

In this lesson you'll learn how to multiply decimals by ignoring the point first, then placing the decimal correctly in the answer.

## The Concept

**Multiplying decimals**: First multiply as if they were whole numbers, then count the total decimal places in both factors and place that many in the product.

Example: $1.2 \times 0.3$

1. Ignore decimals and multiply $12 \times 3$:

$$
\begin{array}{rr}
  1 & 2 \\
  \times & 3 \\
  \hline
  3 & 6
\end{array}
$$

2. Total decimal places: 1 (from 1.2) + 1 (from 0.3) = 2
3. Place decimal 2 places from right: $0.36$

Another: $2.5 \times 4 = 10.0$ (2.5 has 1 place, 4 has 0 → 1 place in the answer)

## Worked Example

Multiply $3.4 \times 0.25$.

1. Ignore decimals and multiply $34 \times 25$:

$$
\begin{array}{rrrr}
  & \scriptsize{1} & & \\
  & & 3 & 4 \\
  \times & & 2 & 5 \\
  \hline
  & 1 & 7 & 0 \\
  & 6 & 8 & 0 \\
  \hline
  & 8 & 5 & 0
\end{array}
$$

2. Total places: 1 (from 3.4) + 2 (from 0.25) = 3
3. Place decimal 3 from right: $0.850 → 0.85$

Multiply $1.6 \times 2.5$.

1. Ignore decimals and multiply $16 \times 25$:

$$
\begin{array}{rrr}
  & \scriptsize{3} & \\
  & 1 & 6 \\
  \times & 2 & 5 \\
  \hline
  & 8 & 0 \\
  3 & 2 & 0 \\
  \hline
  4 & 0 & 0
\end{array}
$$

2. Places: 1 + 1 = 2
3. Place decimal: $4.00 → 4$

## Real-World Application

Multiplying decimals calculates costs (2.5 hours × 18.50 dollars/hour = 46.25 dollars), area (room 12.5 ft × 10.75 ft), or sales tax (subtotal 89.99 dollars × 0.08 tax rate = 7.20 dollars tax). It's essential for budgeting or shopping.

:::note[You've Got This]
The trick is to multiply normally and then slide the decimal — count the places carefully. Practice with money (like tips or tax) and always estimate first ($2.5 \times 18 \approx 45$). You'll avoid mistakes quickly.
:::
