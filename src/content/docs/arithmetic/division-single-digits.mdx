## What You'll Learn

Division is the reverse of multiplication. If you know your times tables, you already know your division facts. This lesson makes that connection clear.

## What Division Means

$12 \div 3$ asks: "How many groups of 3 fit into 12?" or equivalently, "3 times what equals 12?"

$$
12 \div 3 = 4 \quad \text{because} \quad 3 \times 4 = 12
$$

The $\div$ sign means "divided by." You can also write it as a fraction: $\frac{12}{3} = 4$.

Key ideas:

- **Division is the opposite of multiplication**: if $a \times b = c$, then $c \div a = b$
- **Dividing by 1 changes nothing**: $8 \div 1 = 8$
- **A number divided by itself is 1**: $6 \div 6 = 1$
- **Zero divided by anything is 0**: $0 \div 5 = 0$
- **You cannot divide by zero**: $7 \div 0$ is undefined (it doesn't work)

## Using Multiplication to Divide

The fastest way to divide is to think of the related multiplication fact:

- $18 \div 3 = ?$ → Think: $3 \times ? = 18$ → Answer: 6
- $42 \div 7 = ?$ → Think: $7 \times ? = 42$ → Answer: 6
- $56 \div 8 = ?$ → Think: $8 \times ? = 56$ → Answer: 7

This is why knowing your times tables makes division almost free.

## Common Division Facts

### Dividing by 2 (Halving)

- $8 \div 2 = 4$
- $14 \div 2 = 7$
- $18 \div 2 = 9$

### Dividing by 5

Results come from the 5s times table:

- $25 \div 5 = 5$
- $35 \div 5 = 7$
- $45 \div 5 = 9$

### Dividing by 9

Use the 9s times table backward:

- $27 \div 9 = 3$
- $54 \div 9 = 6$
- $81 \div 9 = 9$

## What About Remainders?

Sometimes a number doesn't divide evenly. For example:

$14 \div 3 = ?$

$3 \times 4 = 12$ (fits), $3 \times 5 = 15$ (too big). So $14 \div 3 = 4$ remainder $2$.

We write this as $14 \div 3 = 4 \text{ R } 2$.

The remainder is what's left over: $14 - 12 = 2$.

## Worked Examples

**$48 \div 6$**

Think: $6 \times ? = 48$. Since $6 \times 8 = 48$, the answer is 8.

**$35 \div 7$**

Think: $7 \times ? = 35$. Since $7 \times 5 = 35$, the answer is 5.

**$20 \div 3$**

$3 \times 6 = 18$ (fits), $3 \times 7 = 21$ (too big). So $20 \div 3 = 6 \text{ R } 2$.

:::tip[Division = Multiplication Backward]
If division feels hard, go back and strengthen your times tables. Every division problem is just a multiplication problem in disguise.
:::
