## What You'll Learn

In this lesson you'll learn how to find percent increase (growth) and percent decrease (reduction), including the formula and examples.

## The Concept

**Percent increase/decrease** measures change relative to the original amount.

Formula:

$$
\text{Percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}} \times 100\%
$$

- Positive result = increase
- Negative = decrease

Shortcut for increase: Add the percent of original.
Example: 20% increase on 50 = $50 + (0.20 \times 50) = 60$

For decrease: Subtract the percent.
30% decrease on 80 = $80 - (0.30 \times 80) = 56$

## Worked Examples

A salary of 40,000 dollars increases by 8%.

1. Increase amount: $0.08 \times 40{,}000 = 3{,}200$ dollars
2. New salary: $40{,}000 + 3{,}200 = 43{,}200$ dollars
3. Percent increase: $\frac{3{,}200}{40{,}000} \times 100\% = 8\%$

Price of 120 dollars decreases by 25%.

1. Decrease: $0.25 \times 120 = 30$ dollars
2. New price: $120 - 30 = 90$ dollars

## Real-World Applications

Percent increase/decrease appears in raises (5% salary bump), discounts (30% off sale), inflation (prices up 4%), or fuel efficiency changes. It helps understand how much things really change relative to the starting amount.

:::note[You've Got This]
Percent change is just "how much bigger/smaller compared to the original." Multiply the percent by the original, then add/subtract. Check by seeing if the change makes sense (a 50% increase doubles the number). Practice with sale prices or bills and it'll click fast.
:::
