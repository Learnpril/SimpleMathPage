## What You'll Learn

In this lesson you'll learn what exponents mean, the rules for positive/negative/zero exponents, and how to use scientific notation for large/small numbers.

## The Concept

An **exponent** shows repeated multiplication: $4^3 = 4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$

Rules:

- $a^1 = a$
- $a^0 = 1$ (any non-zero number to power 0)
- $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$ (negative exponent = reciprocal)

Example:

$$
2^{-3} = \frac{1}{2^3} = \frac{1}{8}
$$

Multiplying powers: $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$

Dividing: $a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}$

Power of a power: $(a^m)^n = a^{m \times n}$

**Scientific notation**: Write numbers as $a \times 10^b$ where $1 \leq |a| < 10$.

- $4500 = 4.5 \times 10^3$
- $0.00032 = 3.2 \times 10^{-4}$

## Worked Example

$$
\begin{aligned}
3^4 &= 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 81 \\
5^0 &= 1 \\
2^{-2} &= \frac{1}{2^2} = \frac{1}{4} \\
6 \times 10^5 &= 600{,}000
\end{aligned}
$$

Simplify $4^3 \times 4^2$:

$$
4^3 \times 4^2 = 4^{3+2} = 4^5 = 1024
$$

## Real-World Application

Exponents appear in growth (population doubles = $\times 2$ each period), interest (compound = $P \times (1 + r)^t$), scientific measurements ($3.2 \times 10^{-4}$ grams), or computing ($2^{10}$ bytes = 1 kilobyte). Understanding powers helps with large/small numbers and patterns.

:::note[You've Got This]
Exponents are just "repeated multiplying" — positive = big numbers, negative = fractions, zero = 1. The rules are shortcuts for multiplying/dividing powers. Practice with small exponents and real examples (growth, measurements) — it becomes intuitive fast.
:::
