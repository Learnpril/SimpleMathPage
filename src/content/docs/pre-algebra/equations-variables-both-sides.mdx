## What You'll Learn

In this lesson you'll learn how to solve equations where the variable is on both sides by collecting like terms and using inverse operations to isolate the variable.

## The Concept

**Equations with variables on both sides** have the variable in terms on the left and right (e.g., $3x + 5 = 2x + 12$).

To solve:

1. Move all variable terms to one side (usually left) by adding or subtracting the same term from both sides.
2. Move constants to the other side.
3. Simplify and solve the resulting one- or two-step equation.

Key rule: Whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Keep the equation balanced.

Example:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + 5 &= 2x + 12 \\
3x - 2x + 5 &= 12 \\
x + 5 &= 12 \\
x &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

Check: $3(7) + 5 = 26$ and $2(7) + 12 = 26$. Equal, correct.

## Worked Examples

Solve $5x - 8 = 2x + 7$

$$
\begin{aligned}
5x - 8 &= 2x + 7 \\
5x - 2x - 8 &= 7 \\
3x - 8 &= 7 \\
3x &= 15 \\
x &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Check: Left: $5(5) - 8 = 17$. Right: $2(5) + 7 = 17$. Matches.

Another: Solve $4(y + 3) = 2y + 18$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(y + 3) &= 2y + 18 \\
4y + 12 &= 2y + 18 \\
2y + 12 &= 18 \\
2y &= 6 \\
y &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Check: $4(3 + 3) = 4(6) = 24$ and $2(3) + 18 = 24$. Matches.

## Real-World Applications

These equations solve problems with two related amounts:

You have two savings plans. Plan A: 200 dollars plus 50 dollars per month. Plan B: 100 dollars plus 70 dollars per month. When will they equal?

$$
\begin{aligned}
200 + 50m &= 100 + 70m \\
200 - 100 &= 70m - 50m \\
100 &= 20m \\
m &= 5 \text{ months}
\end{aligned}
$$

Two phone plans. Plan 1: 40 dollars base + 0.05 dollars per text. Plan 2: 30 dollars base + 0.10 dollars per text. When are they equal?

$$
\begin{aligned}
40 + 0.05t &= 30 + 0.10t \\
40 - 30 &= 0.10t - 0.05t \\
10 &= 0.05t \\
t &= 200 \text{ texts}
\end{aligned}
$$

These help compare options, set goals, or find break-even points in budgeting or work.

:::note[You've Got This]
The key is moving the variable terms to one side first. It's like gathering all the x's together. Choose the smaller coefficient to subtract (keeps numbers smaller). Work step by step, check your answer by substituting back, and practice with real comparisons (plans, costs). You'll solve these naturally soon.
:::
