## What You'll Learn

In this lesson you'll learn what "like terms" are and how to combine them to simplify algebraic expressions.

## The Concept

**Like terms** have the same variable raised to the same power (e.g., $5x$ and $3x$, or $7$ and $2$ — constants are like terms with no variable).

To **combine like terms**: Add or subtract their coefficients, keep the variable the same.

Examples:

- $5x + 3x = 8x$
- $12y - 7y = 5y$
- $4 + 9 - 2 = 11$ (constants only)
- $6a + 2b - 4a + 5b = (6a - 4a) + (2b + 5b) = 2a + 7b$

Only combine terms that match exactly — $3x$ and $3x^2$ are not like terms.

## Worked Example

Simplify $7x + 4 + 2x - 9 + 5y$.

1. Group like terms: $(7x + 2x) + 5y + (4 - 9)$
2. Combine: $9x + 5y - 5$

Final simplified expression: $9x + 5y - 5$

## Real-World Application

Combining like terms helps simplify real costs or totals. Example: Weekly expenses = 45 dollars food + 30 dollars gas + 15 dollars snacks + 20 dollars coffee. Group: $(45 + 15)$ food/snacks + 30 gas + 20 coffee = 60 dollars food/snacks + 30 gas + 20 coffee. Or total pay = 18 dollars/hour $\times$ $h$ hours + 50 dollars bonus — leave as is, but if multiple rates, combine like terms.

:::note[You've Got This]
Think of like terms as "same category" items — you can only add apples to apples. Write all terms down, circle the matching ones, and combine coefficients. Start with simple expressions and you'll simplify quickly.
:::
