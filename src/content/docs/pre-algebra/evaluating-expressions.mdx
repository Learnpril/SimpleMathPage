## What You'll Learn

In this lesson you'll learn how to evaluate algebraic expressions by replacing variables with given numbers and following the order of operations.

## The Concept

**Evaluating an expression** means plugging in a specific value for each variable and computing the result, just like following a recipe with exact amounts.

Steps:

1. Replace every instance of the variable with the given number (use parentheses if needed to avoid confusion).
2. Follow order of operations: Parentheses → Exponents → Multiply/Divide (left to right) → Add/Subtract (left to right).

Example: Evaluate $2x + 3y$ when $x = 5$ and $y = 4$

$$
2(5) + 3(4) = 10 + 12 = 22
$$

Another: $5 + 4n$ when $n = 3$

$$
5 + 4(3) = 5 + 12 = 17
$$

## Worked Examples

Evaluate $7 - 2a + b$ when $a = 6$ and $b = 9$

1. Substitute: $7 - 2(6) + 9$
2. Multiply first: $7 - 12 + 9$
3. Add/subtract left to right: $-5 + 9 = 4$

Final result: $4$

## Real-World Applications

Evaluating expressions helps calculate costs or totals when you know the values. Example: Monthly phone bill = 30 dollars base + 0.05 dollars per text. If you send 400 texts: $30 + 0.05(400) = 30 + 20 = 50$ dollars. Or fuel cost = 3.50 dollars per gallon × _g_ gallons. Plug in _g_ = 12 for 42 dollars.

:::note[You've Got This]
Evaluating is just "follow the recipe with real numbers." Write out each step, use parentheses around substituted values, and double-check order of operations. Practice with simple bills or budgets and it'll feel like second nature soon.
:::
