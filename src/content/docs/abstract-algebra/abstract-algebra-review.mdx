## Group Theory

### The four axioms

$(G, *)$ is a **group** when it is closed, associative, has an identity, and every element has an inverse. Commutativity is **not** required; groups that have it are **abelian**.

Consequences from the axioms alone:

- The identity is unique.
- Inverses are unique.
- Cancellation holds: $ab = ac \implies b = c$.
- $(ab)^{-1} = b^{-1}a^{-1}$ - the order reverses.
- $(a^{-1})^{-1} = a$.

### The reference groups

<GroupZoo />

Element orders in $\mathbb{Z}_n$:

$$
|k| = \frac{n}{\gcd(k, n)}
$$

so $k$ generates $\mathbb{Z}_n$ exactly when $\gcd(k,n) = 1$, and there are $\varphi(n)$ generators.

### Subgroups

Test: nonempty, closed under the operation, closed under inverses. Or in one step, $ab^{-1} \in H$ for all $a,b \in H$. **Never re-check associativity**; it's inherited.

A **finite** nonempty subset closed under the operation is automatically a subgroup.

A cyclic group of order $n$ has exactly one subgroup per divisor of $n$. Other groups can have many more: $D_4$ has ten subgroups against $\mathbb{Z}_8$'s four.

### Cosets and Lagrange

Cosets partition $G$ into blocks all of size $|H|$, giving

$$
|G| = [G:H] \cdot |H|
$$

Corollaries: element orders divide $|G|$; $a^{|G|} = e$; every group of prime order is cyclic; Fermat's little theorem and Euler's theorem.

**The converse is false.** $A_4$ has order 12 with no subgroup of order 6.

### Normal subgroups and quotients

$N \trianglelefteq G$ means $gng^{-1} \in N$ for all $g \in G$, $n \in N$. Automatic when $G$ is abelian, when $[G:N] = 2$, or for $Z(G)$.

Normality is exactly what makes $(aN)(bN) = abN$ well-defined, giving the quotient group $G/N$ of order $[G:N]$.

A group whose only normal subgroups are $\{e\}$ and itself is **simple**.

### Homomorphisms

A **homomorphism** satisfies $\varphi(ab) = \varphi(a)\varphi(b)$. Then $\varphi(e) = e$ and $\varphi(a^{-1}) = \varphi(a)^{-1}$ come free.

- $\ker\varphi \trianglelefteq G$, and every normal subgroup is some kernel.
- $\operatorname{im}\varphi \le H$, generally not normal.
- $\varphi$ injective $\iff \ker\varphi = \{e\}$.
- Every fibre has size $|\ker\varphi|$.

**First isomorphism theorem:**

$$
G/\ker\varphi \cong \operatorname{im}\varphi, \qquad |G| = |\ker\varphi|\cdot|\operatorname{im}\varphi|
$$

### Proving groups are or aren't isomorphic

To prove **isomorphic**, exhibit a map. To prove **not isomorphic**, name one differing invariant: order, abelian or not, the multiset of element orders, cyclic or not, number of subgroups, size of the center.

Counts of groups by order: 1 each for prime orders; 2 for order 4; 2 for order 6; **5** for order 8; 5 for order 12; 14 for order 16.

**Fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups:** every one is a direct product of cyclic groups of prime-power order, uniquely up to reordering.

$$
\mathbb{Z}_m \times \mathbb{Z}_n \cong \mathbb{Z}_{mn} \iff \gcd(m,n) = 1
$$

### Permutations and symmetry

$S_n$ has $n!$ elements; $A_n$ has $n!/2$. Cycle notation: order is the lcm of the cycle lengths, parity is the sum of $(k_i - 1)$.

$D_n$ has $2n$ elements, presented as

$$
\langle r, s \mid r^n = s^2 = e,\ sr = r^{-1}s\rangle
$$

Every $r^ks$ is a reflection of order 2. The cube's rotation group has 24 elements and is isomorphic to $S_4$.

**Cayley's theorem:** every group embeds in a symmetric group.

## Rings and Fields

<RingLadder />

### Key definitions

- **Ring** - $(R,+)$ abelian group; $\times$ closed and associative; distributive.
- **Zero divisor** - nonzero $a$ with $ab = 0$ for some nonzero $b$. Zero divisors break cancellation.
- **Integral domain** - commutative, with unity, no zero divisors.
- **Field** - every nonzero element is a unit.
- **Ideal** - additive subgroup that **absorbs** multiplication by all of $R$.

Facts holding in every ring: $0a = 0$, $(-a)b = -(ab)$, $(-a)(-b) = ab$.

### The modular arithmetic summary

In $\mathbb{Z}_n$, an element $a$ is:

- a **unit** if $\gcd(a,n) = 1$ - these form $U(n)$, of size $\varphi(n)$
- a **zero divisor** otherwise (for $a \ne 0$)

$$
\mathbb{Z}_n \text{ is a field} \iff n \text{ is prime}
$$

### Ideals and quotient rings

$R/I$ is a ring when $I$ is an ideal, and absorption is exactly what makes the multiplication well-defined.

$$
I \text{ prime} \iff R/I \text{ a domain}, \qquad I \text{ maximal} \iff R/I \text{ a field}
$$

**The key construction:** for $F$ a field and $f$ **irreducible**, $F[x]/\langle f\rangle$ is a field. This builds $\mathbb{C}$ as $\mathbb{R}[x]/\langle x^2+1\rangle$ and every finite field.

A field has only two ideals, so there is nothing to quotient by.

### Polynomials

Over an integral domain, $\deg(fg) = \deg f + \deg g$. $R[x]$ is a domain exactly when $R$ is, and never a field.

Division algorithm needs a **field** of coefficients. Factor theorem: $f(a) = 0 \iff (x-a) \mid f$. A degree-$n$ polynomial over a field has at most $n$ roots.

Irreducibility is **relative to the field**. Degree 2 or 3 is irreducible exactly when there is no root; from degree 4 that fails, as $x^4+4$ shows.

### Finite fields

$GF(q)$ exists $\iff q = p^n$, and then it is unique up to isomorphism. Build it as $\mathbb{F}_p[x]/\langle f\rangle$ for irreducible $f$ of degree $n$.

$GF(p^n) \ne \mathbb{Z}_{p^n}$ once $n > 1$: $GF(4)$ has characteristic 2 and $\mathbb{Z}_4$ is not a field.

$GF(q)^*$ is cyclic of order $q-1$, so $x^q = x$ for every element.

### Field extensions

$[K:F]$ is a vector-space dimension. $[F(\alpha):F] = \deg m(x)$ for $m$ the minimal polynomial.

**Tower law:** $[L:F] = [L:K]\cdot[K:F]$ - degrees multiply.

Constructible numbers have degree $2^k$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, which is why doubling the cube and trisecting the angle are impossible, and $\pi$ being transcendental settles squaring the circle.

The general quintic is unsolvable by radicals because $S_5$ is not solvable, since $A_5$ is simple and non-abelian.

## The Named Theorems

| Theorem                                  | Statement                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Lagrange                                 | $\|H\|$ divides $\|G\|$, and $\|G\| = [G:H]\cdot\|H\|$                   |
| Cayley                                   | Every group embeds in a symmetric group                                  |
| Cauchy                                   | If a prime $p$ divides $\|G\|$, then $G$ has an element of order $p$     |
| First isomorphism                        | $G/\ker\varphi \cong \operatorname{im}\varphi$                           |
| Fundamental thm of finite abelian groups | A direct product of prime-power cyclic groups, uniquely                  |
| Fermat's little                          | $a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p$ for $\gcd(a,p)=1$                             |
| Euler                                    | $a^{\varphi(n)} \equiv 1 \pmod n$ for $\gcd(a,n)=1$                      |
| Classification of finite fields          | A field of order $q$ exists iff $q$ is a prime power, and is then unique |
| Tower law                                | $[L:F] = [L:K]\cdot[K:F]$                                                |
| Abel–Ruffini                             | No radical formula for the general quintic                               |

## Common Mistakes

- **Writing a set and calling it a group.** Always name the operation. "Is $\mathbb{Z}$ a group?" has no answer.
- **Reading $a^{-1}$ as a reciprocal.** In $(\mathbb{Z},+)$ the inverse of 5 is $-5$.
- **Confusing associativity with commutativity.** Parentheses versus order.
- **Assuming the converse of Lagrange.** A divisor need not be a subgroup order.
- **Forgetting the order reverses** in $(ab)^{-1} = b^{-1}a^{-1}$.
- **Thinking a coset is a subgroup.** Only $eH = H$ is.
- **Reading normality as $gng^{-1} = n$.** It's $gng^{-1} \in N$.
- **Concluding two groups are isomorphic because the invariants match.** You must build the map.
- **Assuming $GF(p^n) = \mathbb{Z}_{p^n}$.** Different characteristic; the second usually isn't a field.
- **Calling a polynomial irreducible without naming the field.**
- **Cancelling in a ring with zero divisors.** In $\mathbb{Z}_6$, $2\cdot1 = 2\cdot4$.
- **Confusing a subring with an ideal.** An ideal must absorb multiplication by everything.
