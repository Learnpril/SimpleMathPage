Mom's Basement University is a free math site for adults who want to learn or relearn
mathematics properly, without a classroom, a deadline, or anyone watching.

## Who this is for

People who never got a fair shot at math the first time, and people who did fine but
forgot most of it. There is no placement test and no assumed starting point. The
Foundations group genuinely begins at counting.

It is also for people who already know some math and want a specific tool: how
quaternions work, why a shader needs a dot product, what backpropagation computes.
That is what the Applied track is for.

## How it is organised

**[Core Mathematics](/core-mathematics/)** is the rigorous track: eighteen subjects in
learning order, from arithmetic through topology. Every lesson has worked examples and
a quiz, and the upper-division subjects prove their results rather than asserting them.

**[Applied Mathematics](/applied-mathematics/)** is the practical track: six subjects
aimed at a craft - games, 3D, shaders, AI and more. Applied is structured as a guide
and reference rather than a lesson sequence. There are no quizzes, and the emphasis is
on showing how the math works in context rather than building it from axioms.

Core is a sequence. Applied is a set of entry points that lean on Core and link back
to it wherever they need something proved.

## How the lessons are built

Every lesson follows the same shape: what you will learn, the concept, worked examples,
where it shows up in the real world, and a quiz. Figures are drawn from computed values
rather than by hand, so a diagram cannot quietly disagree with the text beside it.

Progress is tracked per lesson. It works without an account using local storage, and
syncs across devices if you sign in.

## What it costs

Nothing. There are no ads, no subscription, no upsell, and no email capture. Signing in
exists so your progress follows you between devices, and that is the only thing it does.

:::note[Grab some scratch paper]
The single biggest difference between reading math and learning math is working the
examples by hand before looking at the solution. It is slower and it is the whole point.
:::
